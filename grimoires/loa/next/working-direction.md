# Loa-Arcturus — Revised Working Direction

## Core problem

Berachain needs to attract and retain productive projects, businesses, builders, and economic activity.

Traditional ecosystem acquisition often asks projects to migrate, redeploy, move liquidity, or create chain-specific activity in exchange for grants or incentives.

For already-operating businesses, those requirements may be unattractive because their users, revenue, liquidity, infrastructure, and integrations already exist elsewhere.

## Working hypothesis

Berachain may be able to acquire stronger and more durable economic relationships with external revenue-generating projects by offering growth capital without requiring full migration.

A participating project could remain primarily deployed on another chain or off-chain while agreeing to create a measurable economic relationship with Berachain through some combination of:

- repayment;
- revenue sharing;
- HONEY usage or settlement;
- BERA/sWBERA-directed economic return;
- a limited Berachain-native economic endpoint;
- other contractually defined forms of measurable value.

The central question is not whether a project “moves to Berachain.”

It is:

> **Can Berachain provide capital to a productive external business and receive more durable economic value in return than it would obtain through ordinary migration or incentive programs?**

## Possible role of Loa-Arcturus

Loa-Arcturus is the hypothesis that repeated attempts to create these relationships may reveal missing operational infrastructure.

Its role must be discovered from real deals rather than assumed in advance.

Possible functions include:

- project origination and diligence;
- facility administration;
- evidence collection;
- use-of-proceeds monitoring;
- cross-chain revenue monitoring;
- repayment and revenue-share servicing;
- covenant monitoring;
- measurement of value returned to Berachain;
- portfolio and Return Ledger reporting;
- integration with existing capital, payment, wallet, and settlement systems.

Loa-Arcturus should not become a protocol merely because these functions can be modeled.

The first deals should use ordinary contracts, wallets, databases, scripts, accounting systems, and existing blockchain infrastructure wherever possible.

Reusable infrastructure is justified only when repeated independent deals expose the same operational failure or interoperability need.

## Capital model

Do not assume Berachain must supply all principal directly.

Research and test structures including:

- direct growth facilities;
- revenue-based financing;
- fixed repayment;
- first-loss support;
- guarantees;
- matching capital;
- private senior capital combined with ecosystem support;
- performance-linked incentives.

PoL Next/ERA is one possible capital source or compatibility path where authorized, not an assumption or dependency.

## Agent extension

Autonomous agents are not the architectural center of Loa-Arcturus.

However, economically autonomous software may create an important future requirement.

A company or project receiving a facility may delegate bounded authority to agents to draw or spend capital for approved purposes such as:

- compute;
- APIs;
- inventory;
- advertising;
- gas;
- services;
- other operating expenses.

The initial model should be:

> **The accountable company or controller is the obligor; the agent is an authorized economic operator.**

A future facility may therefore need machine-readable controls covering:

- draw limits;
- spending limits;
- permitted counterparties;
- permitted assets;
- permitted purposes;
- expiry;
- subdelegation;
- revocation;
- repayment sources;
- audit evidence.

This is a MUST-PRESERVE design implication, not current authorization to build an agent-credit protocol.

Promote it into active scope only when real operators demonstrate that prepaid budgets materially constrain productive agent activity and that access to bounded working capital would change their economics.

## Evidence boundary

Public documentation, market analysis, architecture research, and AI synthesis can establish plausibility.

They cannot establish:

- whether Berachain will support the structure;
- whether external projects will accept the terms;
- whether private capital will participate;
- whether the required Berachain economic relationship is attractive;
- whether Loa-Arcturus infrastructure is actually necessary.

Those questions require direct stakeholder evidence and real experiments.

## Current objective

The immediate objective is therefore not to build Loa-Arcturus.

It is to validate one economically compelling transaction in which:

1. a productive external project remains primarily where it already operates;
2. it receives growth capital connected to Berachain;
3. it accepts defined economic obligations in return;
4. Berachain receives measurable, non-circular economic value;
5. the relationship can be operated and serviced in practice.

Then repeat with an independent second project.

Only after observing what repeatedly breaks should Loa-Arcturus's durable product category be decided.

## Falsification

The external-project thesis should be narrowed or killed if:

- strong projects do not want the financing;
- acceptable terms require Berachain to systematically underprice risk;
- Berachain receives only cosmetic or circular activity;
- capital providers will not participate;
- external revenue cannot be monitored sufficiently for the proposed structure;
- ordinary contracts and software solve the operational problem adequately;
- only 0xHoneyJar would use the resulting infrastructure.

The project must not survive by attaching the Loa-Arcturus name to the next adjacent Berachain problem.

## Working direction in one sentence

> **Loa-Arcturus is investigating whether Berachain can attract productive external businesses through capital-backed economic relationships rather than forced migration, and whether repeated real transactions reveal a need for reusable origination, servicing, evidence, or return-measurement infrastructure.**
